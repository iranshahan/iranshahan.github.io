The story

Fifty-three years, and the names that remain

1925
–19791304 – 1357 in the Iranian calendar

Between 1925 and 1979, Iran lived through fifty-three years of building. Railways crossed the Zagros and the Alborz. The University of Tehran opened its doors. Roads, dams, schools and factories rose across the country, and Iranian women won the right to vote. A nation that had been held back for generations began to picture its own future.

Decades later, a new generation went into the streets asking for that future back. In January 2026 the protests that had spread across the country were met with gunfire. Thousands were killed; human rights groups have confirmed thousands of names, and other estimates run far higher. Many were young. Many were never written on any official list.

Iranians call them javidnaman-e rah-e azadi: the eternal names of the road to freedom. Iran Shahan was made so that both are remembered together: the years in which Iran was built, and the people who died believing it could be free again.

The fifty-three years

What was built, 1925 to 1979

The back of the Iran Shahan coin carries one sentence: ۵۳ سال سازندگی، پیشرفت و اقتدار ایرانی, fifty-three years of building, progress and Iranian strength. The 53 laurel leaves around its rim stand for those years, one leaf for each. These are some of the moments behind them.

  1. 1925

    A new beginning

    Reza Shah is proclaimed king and the Pahlavi era begins. A country worn down by weak government and foreign interference sets out to build a modern state.

  2. 1928

    A bank of its own

    Bank Melli Iran, the national bank, opens its doors, and work begins on a modern civil code and modern courts.

  3. 1934

    The first modern university

    The University of Tehran is inaugurated. In 1937 its first women students walk through the gates.

  4. 1935

    Iran, in its own name

    The government asks the world to stop saying Persia and to use the name Iranians have always used for their country: Iran.

  5. 1938

    From the Caspian to the Gulf

    The Trans-Iranian Railway is completed: 1,392 kilometres of track and 190 tunnels across the Alborz and the Zagros, joining the Caspian Sea to the Persian Gulf. Iranians paid for it themselves, through taxes on tea and sugar. Since 2021 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  6. 1941

    A young Shah

    Mohammad Reza Shah comes to the throne at twenty-one, in the middle of a world war.

  7. 1963

    The White Revolution

    Land reform gives farmland to about 1.5 million peasant families. The Literacy Corps sends young graduates to teach in the villages, and the Health Corps sends doctors. Women win the right to vote, and six women are elected to parliament that same year. Through the 1960s and 70s the economy grows by close to 10 percent a year, and literacy rises from about a quarter of the population to more than four in ten.

  8. 1968

    A woman in the cabinet

    Farrokhroo Parsa becomes Minister of Education, the first woman to sit in an Iranian cabinet.

  9. 1971

    A gate for Tehran

    The Shahyad Tower, today's Azadi Tower, rises at the western entrance of the capital to mark 2,500 years of Iranian monarchy.

  10. 1974

    Asia comes to Tehran

    Tehran hosts the Asian Games in the new Aryamehr Stadium, known today as Azadi Stadium.

  11. 1976

    Counting from Cyrus

    Iran adopts the imperial calendar, which counts from the coronation of Cyrus the Great. The year 1976 becomes 2535.

  12. 2585

    This year

    Counted the same way, this year is 2585. It is the year on the front of the Iran Shahan coin, under پاینده ایران, and the year of our wish.

Questions

Before you buy

Is this an investment?

No. Iran Shahan is a commemorative souvenir. Nobody promises anything about its future price, and you should only buy it because you want to keep it.

Where does the money go?

Half goes straight from your wallet to the foundation, and half to the creator, in the same transaction that sends you the souvenirs. The foundation's address is fixed in the contract and can never be changed; you can see it below the purchase steps and check every payment on Tronscan.

What do the numbers mean?

Each souvenir sold takes the next number in line, starting at #101. Numbers #1 to #100 belong to the creator's share. Your numbers are written into the blockchain record of your purchase. EP is a regular TRON token, so the number belongs to the purchase record, not to a particular unit in your wallet.

Why does my wallet ask twice?

The first request lets the souvenir contract take exactly the USDT for this purchase. The second is the purchase. If you buy again later for the same total or less, you may only see the second request.

What are the network fees?

TRON charges a fee in TRX for every transaction. It goes to the network, not to the creator. One purchase costs the same fee whether you buy 1 souvenir or 50, so buying several at once is cheaper per souvenir.

Can the price change?

The creator can raise the price but never set it below 10 USDT. If the price changes between opening this page and confirming in your wallet, the purchase stops and nothing is charged.

What if I live in Iran?

Please do not buy. Wallets that have been used with exchanges inside Iran can be traced, and this purchase could put you at risk. The best way to help is to share this page with Iranians abroad.

Can I sell my souvenirs?

Yes. EP is an ordinary TRON token: the contract puts no limits on it and takes nothing from later sales. You can send it to anyone, or sell it directly to another person at whatever price you agree between you. There is no official marketplace and no promise that someone will want to buy, so only buy what you would be happy to keep.

Who cannot buy?

Residents of countries whose laws restrict this purchase, including the United States.
